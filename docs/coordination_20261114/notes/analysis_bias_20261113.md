## Scope and conventions

- **Candidate** = the uploaded build "tier p": CD stack of deploy tier 1, CV stack of deploy tier 1, HEX 38-column ridge (no CPSAM multi-target column) plus the cell-route step. Strict five-fold OOF, recomputed here from the lanes' files: CD 6.1737, CV 10.1115, HEX 9.7194, mean 8.6682 (8,954 rows; HEX on 8,952).

- **Bias** here means either (i) a systematic difference between the OOF value and the hidden-test value, or (ii) a systematic error in the predictions.
- **Sources** are named by ID format: old format `NNNN-YY` (4,514 rows) and new format `YYYY-NNNN OD/OS` (4,440 rows). "Phase-1 crop members" are the five September models, not a source.
- All values are MAPE in points unless a % sign is given. "Measured" = a number from the records or from my read. "Inferred" = follows from a measured number plus an assumption that is stated.
- Sampling noise of a 1,000-image test set for the candidate (my read, s05, 4,000 draws): SD of the metric mean CD 0.33 / CV 0.53 / HEX 0.27, SD of the score 0.24 (row-random); 0.41 / 0.59 / 0.27, score 0.28 (donor-clustered). Every bias below except item 1 is smaller than one SD of this noise.

## The top items

**1. The test-set-specific gap.** Submission 1 is the only external data point: test minus OOF was CD -0.062, CV -0.061, HEX +0.395. The CV lane showed that this vector lies inside the sampling noise of 1,000 images (joint tail 0.40; HEX alone z = +1.5, one-sided tail 6.4 %). That statement is about a fresh draw. The second submission is scored on the same hidden images (`docs/BRIEF.md:11`), so the question for the candidate is how much of that realised gap it shares. I drew 6,000 sets of 1,000 rows from the OOF pool and regressed the candidate's gap on submission 1's gap in the same draw: correlation 0.986 / 0.993 / 0.912, slope 0.957 / 0.953 / 0.891, residual SD 0.055 / 0.061 / 0.109 (row-random; donor-clustered draws give the same). So under pure sampling the candidate inherits about HEX +0.35, CD -0.06, CV -0.06 from this test set. The two non-noise explanations in the CV lane's report give the same order for HEX: the label-mix tilt that reproduces +0.395 on submission 1 gives +0.34 on the candidate, and a HEX-only label offset of -3.4 % or +3.6 % gives +0.42. What is measured: the gap of one submission and the error correlation. What is inferred: that the realised gap repeats, which needs the same test images and holds under all three explanations. The candidate's gain over submission 1 on the same rows does not depend on which rows are drawn: -0.139 / -0.093 / -0.394 with SD 0.057 / 0.066 / 0.113 over draws, and it is larger under the label-mix tilt (-0.45 on HEX) because the candidate improves most in the outer HEX label deciles (-1.3 and -2.5 in the first and last decile, +0.2 to +0.5 in deciles 4-7).

**2. Impossible labels and the skew of the OOF mean.** Eleven rows with APE above 100 % make 0.272 of the CD mean; six of them are serials 2020-0644 to 0651 (labels 372-926 where every model reads 1,400-3,000; 0.187 in the ledger). On CV three rows make 0.199, and two of those are the two eyes of donor 2019-0064 (CD 1,053 and 520, CV 0.03 and 0.11, HEX 0), which add 0.181 to the CV mean and 0.906 to fold 2's CV. These rows are scored on CD and CV in OOF and unscored on HEX. A 1,000-image set has no such row in 30 % of draws for CD and 72 % for CV; in those draws the mean is 5.91 and 9.92, against 6.29 and 10.61 otherwise. The OOF mean is unbiased in expectation but sits above the median draw by 0.036 (CD) and 0.121 (CV). Whether the hidden set holds such rows is not known; submission 1's CD and CV near OOF neither show nor exclude one.

**3. Regression to the mean.** Calibration against the prediction is clean: the slope of log label on log prediction is 0.980 / 1.039 / 0.989 and the signed error by predicted decile is flat. Against the label the picture is the usual one for a noisy target: the slope of log prediction on log label is 0.76 / 0.46 / 0.42. This is a property of the predictions, not a fault that a function of the prediction can remove (O-CD-SUB1-CALIB reached the same conclusion for CD). Its consequence for the OOF-to-test comparison is that MAPE moves with the width of the test label distribution. The CV lane's composition search found the HEX label mix to be the only covariate that reproduces submission 1's three gaps with an ordinary-sized shift (KL 0.006, frames with HEX of 0.66 or more from 10.3 % to 13.0 %); no image, detector, segmentation or source covariate does. The test labels are hidden, so this cannot be checked.

**4. Old-format donor grouping.** No donor key spans two folds (0 of 6,768), but the key is `slide_id`, and for the 4,514 old-format rows it equals the ID. Adjacent old-format serials have correlated labels only at gap 1 for CD (0.48 against 0.02 at gap 2), which is the signature of fellow eyes: new-format fellow eyes correlate 0.82 on CD while adjacent new-format slides of different donors correlate 0.05. So a large share of adjacent old-format IDs are two eyes of one donor, and 73 % of old-format rows have such a neighbour in another fold. The fold assignment of single rows is random, which gives a natural experiment: rows whose neighbour is in training have a CD error 0.82 lower than rows whose neighbours are all in their own fold (CI -1.59 to -0.07; 3,293 against 484 rows). For submission 1 my read reproduces the BRIEF values exactly (-0.74, CI -1.62 to +0.01; CV +0.52; HEX +0.39). CV and HEX show no effect for the candidate. What this means for the test depends on a fact not in the records. If the hidden old-format frames come from donors with no eye in training, the OOF CD is optimistic by up to 0.30 (0.03 to 0.59), 0.10 on the score. If the organisers split train and test by the same `slide_id` (the only grouping key they published), hidden old-format frames have a fellow eye in training about as often as in our folds and there is no bias. For new-format donors 2,186 of 2,254 slides have both eyes in training, so the organisers did not split those by row. Submission 1's CD gap (-0.062, SD 0.39 donor-clustered) is 0.85 SD below what full exposure loss would give (+0.27) and 0.1 SD from no effect; it does not decide.

**5. The MAPE-optimal shrink.** Every metric ends in a stretch whose level constant is the MAPE-optimal scalar. Predictions therefore sit below the label on average: by 0.9 % (CD), 2.9 % (CV), 1.4 % (HEX) against the conditional mean, and 0.4 % / 1.5 % / 1.4 % against the median. On OOF the level is at its optimum (global rescaling by 0.98-1.02 only loses; the best factor is 1.000 for all three), and out-of-fold recalibrations of CD all lose (+0.004 to +0.050, O-CD-SUB1-CALIB). The shrink assumes that test labels scatter around the image content with the same level and the same right skew as training labels. Submission 1 used scalars of the same kind and its CD and CV transferred. For HEX the open explanations of the +0.395 gap include a label offset of about 3.5 %, which the score cannot sign. By source the shrink differs: trimmed mean log(prediction / label) is -0.89 % (old) and -0.32 % (new) for CD, -2.29 % and -1.08 % for CV, -0.53 % and -1.16 % for HEX. One global stretch serves both sources.

**6. Source mix.** The two sources have different label levels (old / new mean CD 2,927 / 2,664, CV 0.418 / 0.345, HEX 0.515 / 0.577) and different CD error. CV and HEX error are the same in both, so only CD and the size of the candidate's gain move with the mix. The training mix is 50.4 % old format. Nothing in the records states the test mix; submission 1's test CD is compatible with any old-format share between roughly 0.25 and 0.65.

**7. Platform numerics.** The deploy lane measured the T4 default path against the validated numerics on 200 labelled rows and applied the per-row deviations to the strict OOF: +0.0067 on the score, three quarters of it HEX. My read of the same files adds the signed part: there is no level shift (largest -0.06 %). The measurement is an H200 emulation of the T4 path; on three frames a real T4 agreed with the emulation to 0.001 % (CD), 0.04 % (CV), 0.07 % (HEX).

**8. Selection on reported rows.** The lanes measured what strict nesting removes and the candidate's numbers are the strict ones. What strict nesting does not cover is small where it was measured: the member list of each stack was fixed after looking at five-fold results, and fully nested searches from scratch land within +0.002 to +0.016 (CV), -0.001 to +0.003 (CD) and +0.022 (HEX, a weaker procedure) of the fixed list; the hex6 blend constants (0.28, -0.2084, 0.5989) were set on all rows and cost +0.004 HEX when taken from the other four folds (my read, at the hex6-plus-stretch stage). Not measured: the cumulative effect of choosing model families, pooling, schedules and epochs on the same five folds since October. Submission 1, built the same way up to 10-30, showed no such effect on CD and CV.

## Checked and found clean

- **Fold integrity.** No `slide_id` spans two folds. The five Phase-1 crop members' validation rows equal the Phase II folds (5 of 5, 8,954 rows each). My read s01, s02.
- **SSL pretraining.** Fold-specific and on training rows only for recipe A, DINOv3-B and ViT-L (`family_bb_20261108.py:215-254`, assertion at line 234; `phase2_crossfold_ssl_20261015.py:457,519`); the full-data builds use SSL on the 8,952 training rows. The Phase-1 crop members use no SSL.
- **Checkpoint choice for the later members.** Fixed epochs (epoch 20, 16-20, 8-12, final), no validation pick (`family_bb_20261108.py:336`).
- **Label-free parts.** Pre-only detectors were trained on the external set only, Cellpose-SAM is zero-shot, the simulation nets saw simulated tessellations only.
- **Input parity.** 60 of 60 sampled training frames are pixel-identical between the training memmap and the `.mha` read the way the entry reads it; 700 of 700 sampled `.mha` headers are 1296 x 972, uint8, one channel (my read s07). The platform debug case 0001-19 gives the same outputs as training frame 0 (O-DEPLOY-022).
- **Entry against OOF with fold models.** 120 fold-0 frames, GPU: max relative difference CD 0.100 %, CV 0.047 %, HEX 0.014 % (cell-route notes, 11-10 19:55, the 11-10 stack form). Combine stages of the frozen form on 1,791 fold-0 rows: at most 5.6e-16 (O-DEPLOY-003).
- **Deploy parameters equal the nested procedure with no outer fold (L5).** CD up to 7.2e-5, CV exact, HEX at most 1.5e-13, cell-route exact for the full and tier-1 files (F-VER-001, F-VER-002, O-VERIFY-CV-TIERS, O-VERIFY-HEX-TIERS).
- **Rows dropped.** 46 of the 9,000 raw frames are outside the 8,954-row cache because of ID spelling (trailing blank, hyphen); all are new format with ordinary labels; nothing was dropped by content (my read s03). The 8,825-row subset is only the fit set of the cell-route step (valid features, HEX above zero, not a gate frame); scoring uses all rows.
- **Zero labels.** Two rows (HEX = 0), never in training, unscored on HEX as in the organisers' public evaluator; the platform evaluator itself was not verified (`oof_recipeA_20261027/fold2_zero_policy_addendum.json`).
- **Phase-1 scale on all folds.** Refitted without each fold it moves by at most 0.1 % (my read s10); the verifier's per-fold refit changes the best pipeline by -0.003.
- **Signed error by fold.** No fold stands out once the two HEX-zero rows are set aside (they explain fold 2's CV 10.69).
- **Seeds.** No seed was picked for the adopted members (lesson L3). Two columns rest on one trained seed: the attention-pool HEX column (-0.007 to -0.008 strict) and the single-seed DINOv3 term of the CV tier.

## What could not be established

3. Which of the three explanations of submission 1's HEX gap is true (item 1). The records and the score cannot separate them.
4. That the final evaluation uses the same 1,000 images as submission 1: BRIEF states one hidden set and two submissions; the CV lane's report lists reuse as a caveat. Item 1 rests on it.
5. A held-out score for any full-data member, and the size of project-long selection on the five folds (items 8 and 10).
6. L5 for `params/cellroute_deploy_p.json` (the cell-route parameters of the uploaded tier): never checked against a separate refit with no outer fold (deploy summary, loose ends).
